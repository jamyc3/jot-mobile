Why background noise breaks dictation — and what actually fixes it.

Running water, an exhaust fan, wind on a walk. We put every obvious fix through a controlled benchmark on real Jot recordings. The headline: almost everything you'd try to "clean up" the audio makes transcription worse. Here's the evidence, and the path that doesn't.

The benchmark

How we measured it

36 real English clips from Jot's own recordings became the test set — each with Jot's clean-room transcript as the "known-good" reference.

We synthesized three noises — running water (broadband hiss), an exhaust fan (steady broadband + motor hum), and wind (low rumble with gusts) — and mixed them into every clip at two loudness levels: 10 dB (moderate) and 5 dB (harsh). Then we ran each version through the exact transcriber Jot ships (Parakeet TDT 0.6B, on-device via CoreML) and measured the word error rate against the clean-audio result. Higher = further from what Jot would have written in a quiet room.

Every number below was produced on a Mac in an afternoon, on the owner's real voice — not a paper's dataset. The whole bench re-runs in minutes, so any fix can be A/B'd before a line of app code is written.

Finding 1

Broadband noise is the enemy. Rumble barely matters.

The three noises are not equal. Water and the fan spread energy across the speech band and smother it. Wind sits mostly below speech, where the model already ignores it. The owner's lived experience — "I turned on the water and the transcription was pretty bad" — shows up exactly.

DeepFilterNet at full strength — the best AI denoiser available — nearly doubled the errors (26% → 50%). RNNoise was almost as destructive. The gentle settings (high-pass, mild denoise, skip-silence) only manage to break even. Across 6 denoiser settings × 3 noises × 2 levels, not one ever improved accuracy.

Why? The recognizer was trained on real audio, noise and all. A denoiser removes noise but leaves faint artifacts and strips subtle cues — a kind of audio the model has never heard. It gets more confused by the "cleaned" version than by the honest noise.

This isn't just our bench. A 2025 controlled study tested enhancement in front of four recognizers including Parakeet: the noisy audio beat the denoised audio in all 40 configurations, and Parakeet at 10 dB went from 10.9% → 16.8% error worse after denoising. Independent researchers, opposite side of the world, same conclusion.

Finding 3

Can't a smarter model save us? We tried Whisper. No.

If the audio can't be cleaned, maybe a different recognizer copes better. The obvious candidate is OpenAI's Whisper — trained on 680,000 hours of messy web audio, so it should have "seen it all." We ran Whisper small.en on the same noisy clips and measured how much each model drifts from its own clean transcript.

Parakeet — the smaller model Jot already ships — degraded less than Whisper in every noisy condition. So "swap in a famous model" is another dead end; it would make Jot worse in noise. Newer Parakeet checkpoints (v2 → v3) also showed no noise-robustness gain. The lever isn't which off-the-shelf model — it's whether the model was trained on the noise you actually face.

The path forward

So how do you improve on steady sounds?

Water, fans, HVAC, a walk in the wind — these are stationary sounds: steady and predictable. That's exactly what makes them tractable, if you attack them in the right place. Ranked by evidence and leverage:

1

Fix the capture, not the recordingdevice-side

The single highest-leverage lever, and the one thing we couldn't test on a Mac. The phone's own microphone stack (Apple's Voice Processing) can cancel steady noise before it ever corrupts the signal the recognizer reads — no artifacts, because nothing was "cleaned" after the fact. Critical caveat from the research: enable it with auto-gain turned off (isVoiceProcessingAGCEnabled = false) or in measurement mode, or its gain-pumping hurts more than it helps. Also: a mic pointed away from the noise, and AirPods — which sit near the mouth and win on proximity alone.

2

Teach the recognizer the noiseoffline research → ship a checkpoint

The only artifact-free way to genuinely improve accuracy. Fine-tune Parakeet on speech mixed with real water/fan/wind (multi-condition training). The model learns to hear through the noise instead of being tripped by it — and, done right, clean-audio accuracy is unharmed. This is what the literature shows actually works, and it's why Whisper (trained on messy data) was already decent: we just need that same idea, aimed at Jot's specific noises. Ships as a better on-device model, invisibly.

3

Skip-silence (VAD) — tested, and it's a washtested · inconsistent

The textbook fix for "it typed something I never said" is to gate out non-speech (voice-activity detection). We tested it hard — and the reason it doesn't help is good news: fed six clips of pure fan/water noise with no speech, Parakeet correctly wrote nothing, with or without VAD. Unlike Whisper, Parakeet doesn't hallucinate on noise — so there's little for VAD to clean. And on the hardest case, broadband water, the VAD misfires and clips real speech ("My name is Vineet Sriram…" got eaten). Same verdict as the denoisers: inconsistent, and risky where it counts.

4

Be honest in the UXproduct

Broadband noise physically masks speech — past a point, no software recovers it. A quiet "sounds noisy here" hint, or nudging toward AirPods, respects the user more than silently mangling their words.

✕

Proven dead ends — don't build theseclosed

Any bolt-on denoiser (RNNoise, DeepFilterNet, spectral). A high-pass filter for accuracy. A newer Parakeet checkpoint. Swapping to Whisper. All measured here, twice over with the literature. The value of this study is as much what it saves you from building as what it points toward.

For the record

Method & honest caveats

Enough detail to reproduce, and the places where these numbers should be read with care.

RecognizerParakeet TDT 0.6B v3, int8, via FluidAudio/CoreML — the exact engine Jot ships
Test set36 real English clips from Jot recordings (~960 reference words)
ReferenceEach clip's own clean transcript — so a number = how far noise pushes the result off, not absolute accuracy
NoiseSynthesized wind / water / exhaust-fan, mixed at 5 & 10 dB SNR (active-speech referenced)
Denoisersffmpeg high-pass, afftdn, anlmdn, RNNoise (arnndn); DeepFilterNet 3 at full & mild strength
ComparatorsWhisper small.en (whisper.cpp); Parakeet v2 vs v3

Read these with care: most of the noise is synthetic, not field recordings — great for controlled, repeatable A/Bs, but real reverberant water and gusty walking-wind will differ. Noise here degrades speech mostly by masking (wrong/missing words); a dedicated test confirmed Parakeet doesn't hallucinate on pure noise (it stays silent), so that failure mode — Whisper's weakness — isn't Jot's. And scores are drift-from-clean, not absolute. The two real recordings validate the direction, but they're short and mild; the honest next step is more real water/fan/wind clips through the same bench — it's built and takes minutes.
